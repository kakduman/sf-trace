# Muni Metro vs. bus ridership, Jul-Aug 2024 to Jul-Aug 2026

Research notes for the Beta3 backcast. Compiled 2026-10-05. "Repo data" means
`data/beta3/raw/obs/muni_ridership_by_route.csv` (SFMTA Transtat route table, accessed
2026-10-04), `data/beta3/raw/obs/muni_systemwide_ridership.csv` (SFMTA mode table), and the
GTFS feeds `data/beta3/raw/gtfs/muni-2024.zip` (valid 2024-06-22..2024-08-16) and
`data/beta3/raw/gtfs/muni.zip` (valid 2026-07-23..2026-08-28). [INFERENCE] marks a
conclusion drawn from data patterns without a published SFMTA statement.

## 1. Summary

1. **The Aug 2024 base month is depressed for K, M (and S) by a Twin Peaks Tunnel
   closure.** Aug 22-29, 2024 (6 of 22 weekdays) the subway ran only Embarcadero-Castro;
   K, L and M were bus shuttles to Church; West Portal and Forest Hill were closed.
   Source: https://www.sfmta.com/travel-updates/twin-peaks-tunnel-upgrade-work-thursday-august-22-thursday-august-29-2024
   and https://www.sfmta.com/projects/twin-peaks-tunnel-upgrades (both accessed 2026-10-05; the
   project page also lists 2024 weekend closures June 22-23 and July 20-21, which do not affect
   weekday averages). Repo data: K weekday 13,500 (Jul 2024) -> 10,200 (Aug 2024); M 17,800 ->
   13,200; S Shuttle 3,000 -> 4,800 (S ran the extra Castro-Embarcadero service); the "Other"
   mode row jumped 5,100 -> 9,270 [INFERENCE: the shuttle buses were booked as "Other"].
   **Using July only, K grew +3.0% and M +2.8% raw (2024-07 -> 2026-07), i.e. below the
   system.** The reported M +17% and most of the K part of K/T +24% come from August.
2. **T Third and N Judah grew for real in both months**: T +39.5% (Jul) / +40.5% (Aug); N
   +31.5% / +25.9%; J +14.7% / +6.8% (raw, repo data). Buses (all non-rail routes): +3.1% (Jul),
   +2.1% (Aug). Scheduled weekday trips were essentially unchanged (GTFS: T 236 vs 236, N 222 vs
   229, K 197 vs 193, M 198 vs 194, J 157 vs 155).
3. **L Taraval was a bus in Jul-Aug 2024 and rail in 2026**, and the L bus is not in the
   route table at all (see section 3). This changes K/M/S boardings at West Portal.
4. **No documented change in SFMTA's rail counting method 2024-2026 was found.** Rail route
   data are APC-based in both periods, but APC coverage of the LRV fleet rose from roughly
   half (LRV4s only) to 100% after the last Breda ran on 2025-11-12 (section 4). The effect on
   comparability is unknown, not shown to be zero.

## 3. The L Taraval effect on K, M and the system total

- Route table: no "L" row from Apr 2020 to Aug 2024; "L Taraval" reappears 2024-09 (700,
  partial month) and 2024-10 (15,000). Jul-Aug 2026: 18,100 / 18,400. (Repo data.)
- The L **Bus** never appears as a route row. The mode table's "Other" row was about 5,000/weekday
  from Dec 2022 to Sep 2024 (Jul 2024 5,100) and fell to about 400 from Oct 2024, when L rail
  returned. [INFERENCE: the L Bus, about 5,000 weekday boardings, was reported only in "Other".
  Not confirmed by SFMTA.]
- Muni Metro mode row = sum of J, K, M, N, T, S route rows in 2024 (Jul 2024: 88,600 published vs
  88,700 summed; Aug: 85,060 vs 85,000), so the S Shuttle counts as rail, the L Bus does not.
- In 2024, L Bus riders heading downtown boarded K, M or S at West Portal and were counted
  there; in 2026 they ride the L. This shift alone should *lower* K and M in 2026 relative to
  2024 [INFERENCE], which agrees with the near-zero July K/M growth.
- Implication: a 2024 vs 2026 "system" total has to treat L (2026), S (2024) and "Other"
  consistently. Route-table sums excluding L and S: Jul+Aug 2024 926,500 -> 2026 992,000
  (+7.1%); including them: 934,300 -> 1,028,500 (+10.1%).

## 4. Counting method (Q3)

- SFMTA route-level page (methodology dated "Page Last Updated: Sunday, October 1, 2023"): data
  "based on automatic passenger counter (APC) data"; excludes historic streetcar and cable car.
  https://www.sfmta.com/reports/average-daily-muni-boardings-route-and-month-pre-pandemic-present
  (accessed 2026-10-05). No methodology change note found.
- SFMTA mode-level page (last updated 2026-07-01) still says ridership is measured "by
  automated passenger counters on buses and by traffic checkers on rail vehicles."
  https://www.sfmta.com/reports/muni-ridership-average-weekday-ridership (accessed 2026-10-05).
  This conflicts with the route page and the audits below; because the Muni Metro mode row
  equals the sum of APC route rows (section 3), the "traffic checkers" wording looks out of date
  [INFERENCE].
- SF.gov Muni ridership page: monthly figures are estimates from "automatic passenger counter
  data, faregate information, revenue collected, and peak load measurements" and are finalized
  annually. https://www.sf.gov/data--muni-ridership (accessed 2026-10-05, no date shown).
- MTC Triennial Performance Audit (FY2019-21, published June 2024), p. ~12: motor coach,
  trolley coach and light rail unlinked passengers are "primarily generated by on-board automatic
  passenger counters"; APCs are on "the newer light rail vehicles (which currently make up about
  half of the fleet...)"; trips without APCs are covered by an "expansion factor" equal to APC
  ridership times (trips delivered / trips with APC data); the old APC system was decommissioned
  and all APC data now come from Conduent CAD/AVL sensors; FTA certification of APCs per mode had
  been submitted for sign-off.
  https://mtc.ca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2024-09/SFMTA_June_2024_Final_Report.pdf
- MTC Triennial Performance Audit (FY2022-24, published June 2025), p. 12: same description
  (APCs on "the newer light rail vehicles (which will ultimately replace all older light rail
  vehicles)", expansion factor for non-APC trips). No methodology change reported.
  https://mtc.ca.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2025-07/FY2025_SFMTA_TDA_Report_June_2025.pdf
- Fleet transition: the 100th LRV4 arrived Dec 2022 (https://www.sfmta.com/blog/100-and-counting-more-new-trains-join-sfmta-fleet,
  2023-03-15); 164 LRV4s in service and the last Breda run on J Church on 2025-11-12
  (https://sfist.com/2025/11/03/muni-is-finally-retiring-the-last-of-the-30-year-old-breda-light-rail-trains/, 2025-11-03).
  So in summer 2024 a large share of rail trips (Bredas, no APC) was expanded, not counted; in
  summer 2026 every LRV has APCs. **UNCERTAIN:** whether this biases 2024 rail counts up or down
  (it depends on whether APC-equipped trips were representative by line, time and consist
  length) is not published. The route page's caveat says results "are subject to change as data
  quality improves." Line-level APC coverage in 2024 was not found.
- Not found: any SFMTA board report, dashboard note or NTD note announcing a rail estimation
  change in 2024-2026. Searched SFMTA report pages, SFMTA blog/press, MTC audits, news. The NTD
  2025 annual data products are due Oct 2026 (FTA) and were not checked.

## 5. Why rail grew faster than buses (Q1)

Ranked by how well the evidence supports each candidate:

1. **Base-period artifact, K and M (well sourced):** the Aug 22-29, 2024 tunnel closure (section 1).
   July-only growth for K and M is about +3%.
2. **Downtown demand rebound, which helps rail more than buses (well sourced as a trend, size
   not attributed):** SFMTA, 2026-07-01: "Downtown rail boardings have risen almost 14% from May
   2025"; Muni Metro reached 126,000 weekday trips in May 2026, a post-pandemic record and 78% of
   May 2019 (https://www.sfmta.com/press-releases/press-release-muni-tops-2019-weekend-ridership ;
   https://www.sfmta.com/blog/muni-ridership-continues-grow-impressive-may-increases, 2026-07-08).
   March 2026: Metro at 74% of 2019, 121,000 weekday trips; reasons cited were downtown rebound,
   events (Giants opening day 2026-03-25), warm weather, gas at $6.13
   (https://www.kqed.org/news/12081663/the-ohtani-effect-and-more-whats-behind-bay-area-transits-comeback, 2026-04-29).
   Repo cross-check: BART exits at the 8 SF stations, Jul/Aug 2026 vs 2024 = 1.289 (downtown 4:
   1.314), close to Metro excluding L/S (+21% Jul, +34% Aug) and far above Muni buses (+2-3%).
   Kastle office occupancy rose only about 9.5% (2024 = 0.913 of 2026), so Kastle understates
   the downtown trip growth seen on BART and rail [INFERENCE].
3. **Reliability and new fleet (SFMTA's stated reason, not quantified):** the same SFMTA
   releases (2026-07-01, 2026-07-08) credit Muni Forward, Fix-It Weeks, the new LRV4 fleet and a
   roughly 60% cut in Market Street Subway delay events vs May 2019. SFMTA Director Kirschbaum
   called the LRV4s "three to four times more reliable than the Bredas" (SFist, 2025-11-03, URL
   in section 4).
4. **T Third / Central Subway ramp-up (data pattern):** T rose steadily from 14,800 (Feb 2023)
   to 19,000 (mid-2024) to 26,500-27,700 (mid-2026) with unchanged scheduled trips (repo data).
   No SFMTA statement isolating the cause was found [INFERENCE: continued Central Subway
   adoption plus downtown, Mission Bay and Chase Center growth].
5. **L Taraval return:** this moves boardings from K/M/S to L. It does not explain K/M/N/T
   growth; it works against K and M (section 3).
6. **Counting change:** none documented; APC fleet coverage did change (section 4). UNCERTAIN.
7. **Train control upgrade, line shortening or rerouting:** none in either window (section 2).

## 6. Recommendations for the backcast

- Do not use Aug 2024 K, M or S as a base (or flag the base month as disrupted). July-only
  values, or July plus a 22/16 rescale of the August K/M weekday average, are cleaner.
- Compare West Portal-corridor rail as a group ({K, M, S, L Bus ("Other")} in 2024 vs
  {K, M, L} in 2026) instead of route by route.
- Treat rail-vs-bus comparability as **probably comparable but not verified** (APC on both,
  with fleet APC coverage changing). If the model misses T and N specifically, look first at
  downtown and Mission Bay attraction (BART +29% suggests downtown trips grew well beyond the
  Kastle ratio), not at methodology.
